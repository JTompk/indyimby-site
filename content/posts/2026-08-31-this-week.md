Two hearings this week — BZA I on Tuesday, the Metropolitan Development Commission on Wednesday — and on paper it's a quiet one: 24 items, zero new filings since last Monday. But the MDC's consent agenda has more in it than the count suggests. Here's what matters.

## The headline: TWG's six stories at 412 West McCarty

**2026-REG-015** is the largest housing item Indianapolis has heard in weeks: a six-story, mixed-use building at 412 West McCarty Street and two Chadwick Street parcels, steps from Lucas Oil Stadium. TWG is proposing 270 dwelling units, roughly 6,500 square feet of ground-floor commercial and retail, and 204 spaces in an integrated garage.

Two things worth noticing. First, this is not a rezoning. The site is already CBD-2 with the Regional Center overlay, so the use is permitted by right and what the Commission is reviewing is Regional Center design approval — massing, frontage, how the building meets the street. That's the entitlement process working the way it should: the zoning already said yes, and the hearing is about how, not whether. Second, the parking. 204 spaces for 270 units is about three-quarters of a space per home, in a location where a resident can walk to the stadium, the convention district, and the Cultural Trail. A decade ago a downtown project at this ratio would have drawn a fight. This week it's on the no-appeal list, recommended for approval.

## The sleeper: the city is buying a catalog of pre-approved house plans

Buried in the policy resolutions, **2026-P-010** authorizes DMD to contract with Opticos Design for up to $252,500 to produce "residential pre-reviewed architectural plans."

If you follow housing policy, Opticos needs no introduction — it's the Berkeley firm that coined the term "missing middle." Pre-reviewed plans are a tool a growing number of cities use to take friction out of small-scale infill: the city commissions a set of house, duplex, and small-multiplex designs, runs them through plan review once, and then anyone building on an eligible lot can pull the design off the shelf and skip most of the architectural and review cost. For the small builders and owner-developers who do the bulk of Indianapolis infill, that is often the difference between a project penciling and not.

The contract doesn't tell us which lot types, which zoning districts, or how many plans. Those details will decide whether this is a brochure or a real accelerant. It's the item on this week's agenda most worth following into next year.

## The pattern: four lots rezoning to D-5 just to be allowed to be residential

Look at the rezoning list and a pattern jumps out. Four Center Township petitions, all going *to* D-5, all on lots under two-fifths of an acre:

- **2026-ZON-062**, 1234 West 30th Street — 0.13 acre, C-3 to D-5, for a single-family house.
- **2026-ZON-063**, 3009 and 3015 North Gale Street — two lots totaling 0.35 acre, SU-1 to D-5, for residential use.
- **2026-ZON-064**, 1004 South Keystone Avenue — 0.11 acre, I-3 to D-5, to legally establish a house that is *already there* and add a detached accessory building.
- **2026-CZN-839**, 947 Eastern Avenue — 0.08 acre, C-3 to D-5, so an existing house can get an attached garage.

None of these is a development story. They are the paperwork you have to file, and the fee you have to pay, when the zoning map says commercial or industrial and the lot has been a house for decades. Keystone is the clearest case: the dwelling exists, the owner wants a garage, and the only path is a full rezoning through the MDC and Council. Our Zoning Gap analysis found that housing of any kind is prohibited on roughly 30 percent of the land we analyzed. These four petitions are what that number looks like one lot at a time.

One is going the other direction: **2026-CZN-831** at 1144 South Belmont Avenue asks to move 0.25 acre from D-5 to C-3 for commercial use.

## Also at the MDC

**2026-MOD-001**, 7536 South Emerson Avenue, Perry Township — HSL Emerson wants to modify the 2023 approval for its D-P site: drop the 55-and-older head-of-household restriction, spread multi-family units across five buildings instead of one, add a clubhouse and pool, and raise the parking floor to 200 spaces. A senior-restricted project converting to general-market housing is worth flagging as a signal about where demand is.

**2026-MOD-004**, 8133 East 96th Street, Lawrence Township — a request to replace a site-plan commitment dated August 22, 1995 with one file-dated August 2025. Thirty-one years is a long time for a piece of paper to control a C-4 parcel on 96th Street.

**2026-CZN-828**, 1170 Kentucky Avenue — 1.569 acres in the floodway fringe, C-1 to C-4. Staff is asking for a continuance for cause to September 16 with a public hearing set for that date. The move from neighborhood commercial to the broadest commercial category on the Kentucky Avenue corridor is one to watch when it comes back.

**2026-ZON-052 / 2026-APP-013**, 8700 West 30th Street — MSD of Wayne Township rezoning 28.5 acres from SU-2 to PK-1 for playgrounds and sport courts, with park-district approval for a comfort shelter and repaving.

Two real-estate and administrative notes: **2026-R-010** raises DMD's contract with Raven Commercial Group to a not-to-exceed of $325,000 for security enhancements at Union Station. And the member roster printed on the agenda shows two vacancies on the nine-seat Commission — one mayoral appointment, one Council appointment.

## BZA I: a thin docket, mostly deferred

Tuesday's Division I agenda is 13 items, but five are being continued to October 6 and one is withdrawn before it starts. What's actually being heard:

- **2026-UV1-022**, 3441 West Washington Street — a use variance for automobile and light-vehicle sales on an I-3 parcel that sits inside both the floodway fringe and the TOD overlay. A car lot on a transit corridor is exactly the kind of use the overlay was written to discourage; it will be instructive to see how the board reads it.
- **2026-UV1-023**, 1002 East 25th Street — Global Preparatory Academy Monon seeks an internally illuminated digital monument sign within 25 feet of a protected district (600 feet required) and two feet from the front line.
- **2026-UV1-021**, 6448 West Ohio Street — Marathon Health asking to run a medical clinic in a government-owned building in Wayne Township. Expedited.
- **2026-DV1-033**, 8006 Meridian Hills Lane — a house addition taking a D-S lot from 85 percent open space to 80. Expedited.
- **2026-UV1-019**, 8185 Sycamore Road — an 864-square-foot playhouse in an established front yard, continued from earlier.

The continued item most worth a second look is **2026-UV1-020** at 3503 North College Avenue: a use variance for vehicle storage and a commercial parking lot on D-5/C-3 ground with zero setbacks, no landscaping, no striping, and a six-foot chain-link perimeter fence with barbed wire — on College Avenue. It's been automatically continued to October 6, which gives neighbors a month to read the file.

Also withdrawn: **2026-DV1-039**, a multi-unit house on a 38-foot lot on North Sherman Drive that needed two feet of lot width and two feet of side setback. Small projects die on small numbers.
